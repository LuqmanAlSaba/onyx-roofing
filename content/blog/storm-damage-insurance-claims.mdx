If you live in Kentucky, you're familiar with the sound of hail hitting your roof or the wind howling during a severe storm. But what happens the next day? Often, it involves a knock on your door from a roofing contractor.

While an unexpected knock on the door can be surprising, these contractors are often responding to real data indicating your home may have been damaged. Understanding *why* they are there can unlock a major opportunity: **a full roof replacement paid for by your insurance company.**

## The Post-Storm Inspection Process

Roofing companies track storm data—specifically hail size and wind speeds—to identify neighborhoods that have likely sustained damage. They target these areas because insurance policies typically cover damage caused by "Acts of God," such as hail or wind.

<ListSection>
- **The Opportunity**: If your roof has sufficient storm damage, your insurance carrier may pay to replace the entire roof, minus your deductible.
- **The Urgency**: Most insurance policies have a limited window (often 1 year) from the date of the storm to file a claim.
- **The Goal**: Contractors want to help you file this claim before the window closes, securing a job for them and a new roof for you.
</ListSection>

## The Insurance Claim Process: Step-by-Step

Navigating an insurance claim can be confusing. Here is the standard process we guide our customers through at Onyx Roofing.

<NumberedList>
- **The Inspection**: A roofer inspects your property for "collateral damage" (dents on gutters, window screens, AC units) and roof damage (bruised shingles, creased tabs).
- **Filing the Claim**: If sufficient damage is found, you call your insurance provider to file a claim. They will assign an adjuster to your case.
- **The Adjuster Meeting**: This is the most critical step. Your roofer should meet the insurance adjuster at your home to point out the damage they found. This ensures nothing is overlooked.
- **Approval & Scope**: If the adjuster agrees with the damage assessment, they will issue a "Scope of Loss" detailing what they will pay to replace.
- **The Build**: You choose your shingles and schedule the installation. Your only out-of-pocket cost should be your deductible.
</NumberedList>

## What Does Storm Damage Look Like?

You might not see damage from the ground. Hail damage often looks like small bruises where granules have been knocked off the shingle, exposing the asphalt mat. Wind damage typically appears as creased or missing shingles.
