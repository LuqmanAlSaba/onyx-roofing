Regular roof inspections are the key to catching small problems before they become major expenses. While professional inspections are essential, knowing how to conduct your own basic inspection helps you stay on top of your roof's condition year-round.

## Why Regular Inspections Matter

Your roof is constantly exposed to harsh elements:

- **UV radiation** degrades materials over time
- **Wind** can loosen or remove shingles
- **Rain** tests your roof's waterproofing
- **Temperature changes** cause expansion and contraction
- **Debris** can trap moisture and cause damage

A small leak can quickly lead to:
- Mold and mildew growth ($2,000-$6,000 remediation)
- Structural damage ($5,000-$15,000 repairs)
- Insulation damage ($1,500-$3,000 replacement)
- Interior damage ($3,000-$10,000+ restoration)

**Prevention is always cheaper than repair.**

## When to Inspect Your Roof

### Recommended Schedule

**Twice yearly** (minimum):
- **Spring** (April-May): After winter weather
- **Fall** (September-October): Before winter arrives

**After major weather events**:
- Severe thunderstorms
- Hail storms
- High winds (40+ mph)
- Heavy snow or ice
- Tornado warnings in your area

**Before and after projects**:
- Before listing your home for sale
- After any roof work or repairs
- Before renewing insurance
- When installing solar panels or satellite dishes

## Safety First

### Ground-Level Inspection

Most inspection tasks can be completed safely from the ground:

**Tools you'll need**:
- Binoculars (10x magnification recommended)
- Camera or smartphone
- Notebook and pen
- Flashlight
- Ladder (for gutter inspection only)

### When to Access the Roof

**Only access your roof if**:
- You're physically capable and comfortable with heights
- Weather conditions are ideal (dry, calm, good visibility)
- The roof pitch is walkable (less than 6/12 slope)
- You have proper safety equipment
- Someone knows you're on the roof

**Never access the roof if**:
- It's wet, icy, or windy
- You're alone
- The roof is steep
- You're uncomfortable with heights
- Shingles appear damaged or loose

## Exterior Inspection Checklist

### Shingles and Roofing Material

**What to look for**:

- [ ] **Missing shingles** — Gaps in coverage expose underlayment
- [ ] **Cracked shingles** — Splits or breaks in material
- [ ] **Curling edges** — Shingles lifting at corners or edges
- [ ] **Blistering** — Bubbles or raised areas on shingles
- [ ] **Granule loss** — Bare spots or excessive granules in gutters
- [ ] **Algae or moss growth** — Dark streaks or green patches
- [ ] **Damaged or missing ridge caps** — Exposed peak of roof
- [ ] **Sagging areas** — Dips or depressions in roof surface

**What it means**:
- Minor issues: 1-5 damaged shingles (repair)
- Moderate issues: 6-15 damaged shingles or widespread granule loss (repair or partial replacement)
- Major issues: Extensive damage, sagging, or age-related deterioration (replacement)

### Flashing

Flashing seals vulnerable areas where water can penetrate:

**Locations to inspect**:

- [ ] **Chimney flashing** — Metal or rubber seal around chimney base
- [ ] **Vent pipe flashing** — Rubber boots around plumbing vents
- [ ] **Skylight flashing** — Seals around skylight frames
- [ ] **Valley flashing** — Metal channels where roof planes meet
- [ ] **Drip edge** — Metal edge along eaves and rakes
- [ ] **Step flashing** — Where roof meets vertical walls

**Warning signs**:
- Rust or corrosion
- Gaps or separation from roof
- Cracked rubber boots
- Missing or damaged sections
- Lifted or bent metal

### Gutters and Downspouts

**Inspection points**:

- [ ] **Debris accumulation** — Leaves, twigs, sediment
- [ ] **Proper attachment** — Securely fastened to fascia
- [ ] **Sagging sections** — Improper slope or support
- [ ] **Rust or holes** — Corrosion or damage
- [ ] **Downspout connection** — Secure and directing water away
- [ ] **Splash blocks** — Present and properly positioned
- [ ] **Gutter guards** — Intact and functioning (if installed)

**Test**: Pour water in gutters to verify proper drainage and check for leaks.

### Soffits and Fascia

- [ ] **Rot or decay** — Soft spots or discoloration
- [ ] **Pest damage** — Holes or nesting evidence
- [ ] **Paint condition** — Peeling or bubbling
- [ ] **Ventilation** — Soffit vents clear and unobstructed
- [ ] **Secure attachment** — No loose or sagging sections

### Chimney

- [ ] **Mortar condition** — Cracks or missing mortar
- [ ] **Brick integrity** — Loose or damaged bricks
- [ ] **Chimney cap** — Present and undamaged
- [ ] **Flashing seal** — Intact and properly sealed
- [ ] **Leaning or tilting** — Structural concerns

### Trees and Vegetation

- [ ] **Overhanging branches** — Within 10 feet of roof
- [ ] **Debris accumulation** — Leaves, needles, or branches on roof
- [ ] **Moss or algae** — Growth on shaded areas
- [ ] **Root damage** — Trees too close to foundation

## Interior Inspection Checklist

### Attic Inspection

**What to bring**:
- Flashlight or headlamp
- Dust mask
- Camera
- Moisture meter (optional)

**Inspection points**:

- [ ] **Water stains** — Discoloration on rafters or decking
- [ ] **Active leaks** — Wet spots or dripping water
- [ ] **Daylight penetration** — Light visible through roof boards
- [ ] **Sagging decking** — Bowed or warped roof boards
- [ ] **Insulation condition** — Dry, evenly distributed, adequate depth
- [ ] **Ventilation** — Soffit and ridge vents clear
- [ ] **Mold or mildew** — Musty smell or visible growth
- [ ] **Pest evidence** — Droppings, nests, or damage
- [ ] **Proper ventilation** — No condensation or excessive heat

**Insulation check**:
- Measure depth (should be 10-14 inches for R-38 to R-49)
- Check for compression or gaps
- Verify it doesn't block soffit vents

### Ceiling Inspection

Walk through every room and look for:

- [ ] **Water stains** — Brown or yellow discoloration
- [ ] **Peeling paint** — Near roof line or corners
- [ ] **Sagging** — Dips or bulges in ceiling
- [ ] **Cracks** — Especially near walls or corners
- [ ] **Mold growth** — Black spots or musty odor

### Wall Inspection

Check exterior walls, especially near roof line:

- [ ] **Water stains** — Discoloration or streaking
- [ ] **Peeling paint** — Moisture damage indicator
- [ ] **Bubbling wallpaper** — Hidden moisture
- [ ] **Mold or mildew** — Visible growth or odor

## Documentation

### Photo Documentation

Take photos of:
- Overall roof condition (all four sides)
- Any damage or concerns
- Problem areas from multiple angles
- Before and after repairs
- Seasonal changes

**Organize photos by**:
- Date
- Location on roof
- Type of issue

### Maintenance Log

Keep a record of:

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Minor Issues (DIY Possible)

**Problem**: Clogged gutters
**Solution**: Clean debris, flush with water

**Problem**: Small amounts of granule loss
**Solution**: Monitor; normal aging process

**Problem**: Minor moss growth
**Solution**: Gentle cleaning with appropriate cleaner

### Moderate Issues (Professional Recommended)

**Problem**: 3-10 damaged shingles
**Solution**: Professional repair or replacement

**Problem**: Small flashing gaps
**Solution**: Professional resealing or replacement

**Problem**: Excessive granule loss
**Solution**: Professional assessment for potential replacement

### Major Issues (Professional Required)

**Problem**: Sagging roof sections
**Solution**: Immediate professional structural assessment

**Problem**: Extensive water damage
**Solution**: Professional leak repair and damage restoration

**Problem**: Widespread shingle failure
**Solution**: Professional roof replacement
